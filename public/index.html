Who reviews whom

Nobody rates themselves. Every person is rated by at least three other people, from different directions. Each rater fills this form in once, about one person.

Review typeWho fills it inWhat it measuresMinimum

Leads are rated by their own reviewer too, so a Channel Manager receives a Lead → Member scorecard from Cenri or Luka, upward reviews from their team, and peer reviews from the other CMs. Partnership contractors (Barbara's and Leo's teams) are reviewed by their partner, per Section 5 of the guide.

Section 3 — Rating Scale

All reviewers must use this standardised 5-point scale consistently across every assessment. Do not apply personal interpretation to the scale — use the descriptions below as the reference point.

RatingLabelDescriptionAction
5Outstanding Consistently exceeds all expectations. Exceptional quality, reliability, and impact. Sets the standard for others. Recognition + bonus consideration
4Exceeds Expectations Regularly goes beyond what is required. Strong performer who delivers quality work ahead of deadlines. Recognition
3Meets Expectations Consistently delivers required output on time and to standard. Reliable and dependable. Continue + set growth goals
2Needs Improvement Inconsistent delivery. Misses deadlines or quality standards regularly. Requires coaching. Performance Improvement Plan
1Unsatisfactory Fails to meet basic expectations. Persistent issues unresolved after support. Contract review / termination
Important note on ratings. A rating of 3 (Meets Expectations) is a GOOD rating — it means the contractor is doing exactly what they were hired to do. Not everyone needs to be a 4 or 5. Reserve 4s and 5s for genuine exceptional performance backed by specific evidence. Never give a 5 just to be nice — it dilutes the meaning for those who truly earn it.
Leave a criterion on N/A if you have not worked with the person closely enough to judge it honestly. A blank is more useful than a guess.
Before you rate — the five common rating errors (Section 5)
ErrorWhat it looks likeHow to avoid it
LeniencyEveryone on the team gets 4s and 5s. Nobody is rated below 3.Justify each rating with a specific example. If you can't, lower it.
Halo effectSomeone you like scores high across ALL criteria, even where evidence is weak.Rate each criterion on its own. One strength doesn't carry the rest.
Recency biasYour ratings reflect only the last 2–4 weeks, not the full period.Deliberately recall events from the start of the review period.
Central tendencyEveryone gets exactly 3 on everything. No variation at all.Very few people are identical in every area. Differentiate honestly.
Personal biasPeople you're friendly with score higher regardless of output.Rate the work, not the friendship. HR checks for this pattern across raters.
These matter more in peer review than anywhere else. HR reviews all submissions for these patterns before any of it is used in a feedback session.

What happens after you submit

Your review is one input of several. HR collects every review this person receives, then blends them: the lead's scorecard at 60%, the peer average at 25%, and the upward average at 15%. If a group doesn't apply, the weights re-balance automatically.

Nobody is finalised on fewer than three raters. Peer and upward comments stay sealed until at least three people in that group have answered — below that the feedback is identifiable. Once a person's set is complete, HR sends the final rating and its KPI breakdown to OrangeHRM.
